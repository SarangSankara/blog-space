"World will end in a few centuries because of humans. We are turning earth into an uninhabitable planet at a faster rate, thereby digging our own grave." - We have heard these types of quotes literally everywhere. Almost everyone we have met has shown some sort of dislike towards the human approach to life in general. A significant percentage of those who are reading this article might be supporting this bias. As social beings, it is impossible for us to not have any bias. This article will highlight how pessimistic and cynical views can lead to problematic situations which would have otherwise been avoidable.

First, let us start off with a seemingly correct statement - "When human beings are met with enormous crisis, their morale will be broken and they will show their true nature." Many of warlords and dictators throughout the history also agreed with the previous statement. Were they correct? Were they able to reap the rewards of war by deploying these tactics? Let us analyze a situation during World War II. Hitler initiated his infamous war technique "Blitzkrieg" and attacked London, which is the heart of Great Britain, to cause trauma on British people and government. The researchers also expected the civilians to be hiding in safehouses and showing humongous panic. But what really transpired was astounding. People were doing their business as usual, and a significant amount of them were helping those who were affected badly by the bombings. Small boys continued to play, people went to work, and the traffic police directed the traffic as usual. They were not afraid of bombings. They knew that it would take some time for the country to retaliate, and tolerated the bombings to an extent. The properties and other tangible stuff got broken, but not their intangible spirits.



After the war, sadness, fury and terrible grief was there. But public health improved quite a bit, with a smaller number of alcoholics around and less suicides. Many people actually yearned for the Blitz days as most people helped each other out to live a better life. Pessimists argued that crisis brought out the worst in people. But by analyzing the previous situation, we can surely say that it brought out the best in people.



There is a pessimistic myth that human beings are generally selfish, aggressive and quick to panic. Most of the readers of this magazine might be of the same view. I would argue that in most cases, human beings act selfless and helps others during a crisis. For example, let us look at some more cases. The sinking of Titanic is shown in movie as an extremely chaotic affair. While in reality, the evacuation was done orderly. Eyewitnesses recollected that there were no signs of fear or hysteria. Twin towers (World Trade Centre) were attacked by a terrorist group in 2001 September 11th (9/11). While the buildings were burning, people were calmly walking down the stairs and extending arms of support to firefighters. People did not selfishly in that scenario as well.



The placebo effect is when an improvement of symptoms is observed, despite using a nonactive treatment. It's believed to occur due to psychological factors. When the doctor gives you fake pills and tells you that you will be better after taking them, chances are you will feel better. Likewise, there is a nocebo effect as well. It hasn't been widely studied. But if you believe in something negative or cynical, chances are it will happen. The grim view on humanity is also a nocebo. The more we think of ourselves as a species destroying the life on earth, the more likely it is going to happen in future.
